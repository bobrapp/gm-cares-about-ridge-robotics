Past champions · FIRST LEGO League Challenge · global

Past Champions: What can we learn?

Ridge Robotics competes in FIRST LEGO League Challenge, the same division as every team on this page. These are the World Festival Champion’s Award podiums for the last three seasons, from Houston, with nine team profiles broken down the way judges score: robot build, software, Innovation Project design, presentation and Core Values. Read it as a playbook, not a scoreboard.

Tutorial

How a Champion’s Award is actually won

The Champion’s Award is not “best robot.” It goes to the team ranked first in the Champion’s Rank, which combines the Robot Game score with the judges’ rubrics for Robot Design, Innovation Project and Core Values; one winner and a small set of finalists are named at each event.24 At the World Festival the field is the national and regional champions of 60-plus countries.1 2

Step 1 · Robot Game

Consistency beats ceiling

Ten teams hit a perfect 545 in 2026; the tiebreaks were about repeating it.6 The Mavericks’ 100 → 355 → 535 arc shows the other half: a repair plan and a spare motor matter as much as the program.14

Step 2 · Robot Design

Show your engineering, not just your robot

Judges praised Robot Rampage’s gear shifting, “pencil-point accuracy” and robust testing methods8; Brazil’s Robot Design winner logged every launch in spreadsheets and filmed runs to review attachments.20 The notebook is the evidence.

Step 3 · Innovation Project

Real experts, real users, real tests

ROBOTECH interviewed archaeologists and attended the professional society’s webinars5; the Mavericks deployed their UV-C rig in Sequim Bay with national-lab scientists15; Robot Rampage’s tool holder has a provisional patent and is on real dig sites.8

Step 4 · Core Values

Help the team at the next table

FSIngenium lent a motor to a rival; both finished as finalists.14 Robot Rampage ran a design forum for other teams.8 Ridge already wins Core Values awards; this is the same muscle on a bigger stage.

Ridge’s path to this list

Qualifier → Eastern Washington State → Houston

In Washington, the state Champion’s Award winner advances to the World Festival; a lottery sends additional strong teams to Open Invitationals, which is how Ridge reached New Jersey in 2026.25 The only Washington team ever on this podium, the Intelligent Mavericks, is a grades 5–8 team from Richland, two hours from Ephrata.12 14

2025–26 · UNEARTHED Champion’s Award winner

ROBOTECH

Mollerussa (Lleida), competing as Barcelona, Spain

Who they are
An independent, student-led STEM team (not a school team) from the small city of Mollerussa; the team describes itself as boys and girls who develop programming and robotics projects together.4
The challenge
The 2025–26 UNEARTHED season asked teams to use technology to explore the past: archaeology, excavation and what artifacts tell us about people. World Festival: 160 teams from 66 countries, Houston, April 29–May 2, 2026.1 13
Innovation Project (design)
ARKEODENS, an automated flotation and sediment-processing system that helps archaeologists recover seeds, tiny bones, shells and charcoal from soil samples by separating material by density and size. They learned flotation is slow, manual and inconsistent, so they automated part of it and built in documentation, explicitly to assist archaeologists rather than replace them. They attended Archaeological Institute of America webinars, visited sites and museums, and interviewed university, museum and heritage experts by video call.5
Robot build
Robot hardware is not described in public coverage, but the result is: 5th in the world in Robot Performance at the Festival, so the robot was both fast and consistent.1
Software
Not publicly documented; ask the team through the channel below.
Presentation & Core Values
The AIA, which fielded a flood of archaeology questions from FLL teams, singled out ROBOTECH for how seriously the students engaged real practitioners.5
Why they scored so highly
The Champion’s Award goes to the team ranked first across all four rubrics. ROBOTECH paired a top-5 robot with an Innovation Project judged by the discipline’s own professional society as genuinely useful. Lesson for Ridge: talk to the actual experts, and let them tell you what is slow and annoying in their work.1 5
Public contact
Team website: robotech.cat (contact form on site). No personal numbers published.

2025–26 · UNEARTHED Champion’s Award finalist

SCRAPS by FutureLab

Kalamaria, Thessaloniki, Greece

Who they are
A team from FutureLab, an educational robotics academy founded by scientists and educators, with Aristotle University of Thessaloniki’s technology-transfer company (ACTA) as strategic partner since 2019. Greek national champions in 2026.6 7
The challenge
The 2025–26 UNEARTHED season asked teams to use technology to explore the past: archaeology, excavation and what artifacts tell us about people. World Festival: 160 teams from 66 countries, Houston, April 29–May 2, 2026.1 13
Innovation Project (design)
Public coverage describes an “innovative proposal” on the archaeology theme that earned top rubric marks, but not the specifics.6
Robot build
A perfect Robot Game: 545 points, which ten teams matched; SCRAPS ranked 9th worldwide on tiebreak. That is every mission, every run.6
Software
Not publicly documented; ask the team through the channel below.
Presentation & Core Values
Top rubric evaluations in Robot Design, Innovation Project and Core Values, per the academy.6
Why they scored so highly
Balance. A maximum robot score plus top marks on all three judged rubrics is exactly the Champion’s Award formula. Lesson for Ridge: the robot ceiling is reachable, but the award is decided in the judging room.1 6
Public contact
Academy website: roboticsacademy.edu.gr · generic email info@futurelab.edu.gr (organization inbox).

2025–26 · UNEARTHED Champion’s Award finalist

Robot Rampage

West St. Paul, Minnesota, USA

Who they are
A five-student middle-school team from St. Croix Lutheran Academy, in its ninth year as a program; the current team credits earlier members for the foundation.8 9
The challenge
The 2025–26 UNEARTHED season asked teams to use technology to explore the past: archaeology, excavation and what artifacts tell us about people. World Festival: 160 teams from 66 countries, Houston, April 29–May 2, 2026.1 13
Innovation Project (design)
Tripodamus, a tool-holding invention that keeps archaeologists from losing tools and straining their backs. It has a provisional patent and is in use at active dig sites.8
Robot build
Judges praised shifting gears, “pencil-point accuracy” and innovative attachments. After technical trouble in two rounds, the team made fixes over the midday break and hit a team-record 500 on the final run.8
Software
Not publicly documented; ask the team through the channel below.
Presentation & Core Values
Ran a design forum to share robot methods with other teams; judges also noted robust testing methods and how the team honored absent teammates. 1st in Core Values at state.8
Why they scored so highly
Outreach that helps rivals, a project with real users, and the composure to repair and re-run under pressure. Lesson for Ridge: build a repair plan into competition day, and share what you know.1 8
Public contact
School: stcroixlutheran.org/msrobot · team site robot-rampage.com.

2024–25 · SUBMERGED Champion’s Award winner

SESI HEROES

Jundiaí, São Paulo, Brazil

Who they are
Students of Escola SESI Jundiaí, part of the SESI-SP industry-run school network, which with this win became a three-time World Festival champion.10 11
The challenge
The 2024–25 SUBMERGED season asked teams to solve problems ocean explorers face, from sensors to habitats. World Festival: 160 Challenge teams, Houston, April 16–19, 2025.2 10
Innovation Project (design)
SmartReef, an automated extractor for removing invasive corals from the seabed, replacing divers’ mallet-and-chisel work that is slow and risky. Invasive coral is a documented problem on the São Paulo coast.10 11
Robot build
Not publicly documented; ask the team through the channel below.
Software
Not publicly documented; ask the team through the channel below.
Presentation & Core Values
Judges cited “exemplary performance in all evaluated categories”; the school highlights teamwork, research and programming.10
Why they scored so highly
A clearly scoped, locally grounded problem with a mechanical solution the team could demonstrate. Lesson for Ridge: pick a problem you can see from your own shoreline (or orchard), and show the device working.2 10
Public contact
School network: jundiai.sesisp.org.br · SESI-SP general line (11) 4210-4455 (organization, not a person).

2024–25 · SUBMERGED Champion’s Award finalist

Intelligent Mavericks

Richland, Washington, USA

Who they are
A grades 5–8 team drawing from Lewis and Clark Elementary, Libby and Enterprise Middle Schools and Three Rivers HomeLink in the Richland School District. The first Washington team ever to reach the Champion’s finalist tier, and the only U.S. team to do so in 2025.12 14
The challenge
The 2024–25 SUBMERGED season asked teams to solve problems ocean explorers face, from sensors to habitats. World Festival: 160 Challenge teams, Houston, April 16–19, 2025.2 10
Innovation Project (design)
EPIC (Epibiosis Preventative Illuminated Contraption), an underwater UV-C lighting rig to stop biofouling on ocean sensors. Built with Pacific Northwest National Laboratory scientists, deployed twice in salt water at PNNL-Sequim and John Wayne Marina, then revised into EPIC Twist after expert feedback. Perfect Innovation Project rubric score.12 15
Robot build
A motor failed in match one (100 points). FSIngenium of Spain lent a spare; the team rebuilt and re-tuned, scoring 355 then 535, ten points from their maximum.14
Software
Reprogrammed mission paths to the replacement motor’s behavior between matches; language and tools not stated.14
Presentation & Core Values
Judges praised confidence, creativity and deep understanding of the science; the team shared its robot and project with attendees.14 15
Why they scored so highly
Real field data from a national lab partnership, plus grace under failure. Lesson for Ridge: PNNL is in Washington; so are WSU Extension, fisheries biologists and orchardists. Ask them. And carry a spare motor.2 14
Public contact
District: rsd.edu · info@rsd.edu · (509) 967-6000 · team site 46665robotics.weebly.com.

Software

2023–24 · MASTERPIECE Champion’s Award winner

Los Atômicos

Araras, São Paulo, Brazil

Who they are
Escola SESI-SP de Araras (CE 303); the program dates to 2012 and the coach credits that legacy. Regional runner-up, national champion, then world champion in one season.18 19
The challenge
The 2023–24 MASTERPIECE season asked teams to explore the role STEM plays in the arts and how people share their passions. World Festival: 152 Challenge teams, Houston, April 17–20, 2024.3 16
Innovation Project (design)
An inclusion and accessibility project: an intelligent chessboard to bring more people, including those with disabilities, into chess. Project electronics: Arduino, a reflectance sensor, speaker and addressable LED strips.18 20
Robot build
Built “to optimize mission time”: SPIKE kit, two large and two medium motors, two color sensors, a gyro, and a pneumatic piston kit for attachments.20
Software
Not stated beyond the sensor set; the gyro and dual color sensors imply heading control and line/edge squaring.20
Presentation & Core Values
Coach: a team of students “with values who respect each other’s opinions,” and a decade of accumulated know-how passed down.18
Why they scored so highly
Everything, consistently: the award text itself says excellence across Robot Performance, Robot Design, Innovation Project and Core Values. Lesson for Ridge: keep the notebook and the attachments from year to year; legacy is a competitive advantage.3 20
Public contact
School: araras.sesisp.org.br · SESI-SP general line (11) 4210-4455 · published coach: Ana Paula Carroci da Silva (reach via the school).

2023–24 · MASTERPIECE Champion’s Award finalist (tied 2nd)

Master Cubz

Andover, Massachusetts, USA

Who they are
A local middle-school team that won the Massachusetts MASTERPIECE state championship before Houston.21
The challenge
The 2023–24 MASTERPIECE season asked teams to explore the role STEM plays in the arts and how people share their passions. World Festival: 152 Challenge teams, Houston, April 17–20, 2024.3 16
Innovation Project (design)
Steering into the Future: a 3D-printed, touchable replica of the Addison Gallery’s model ship Clermont with a button that plays an AI-scripted audio description, so visitors with visual impairments can experience the museum piece.21
Robot build
Robot “Wall-E” ran three clean runs including one maximum score; 5th in Robot Performance worldwide and first among U.S. teams.21
Software
Not publicly documented; ask the team through the channel below.
Presentation & Core Values
The museum itself published the team’s story and credits “warm and cooperative core values.”21
Why they scored so highly
A partner institution that wanted the project, three impeccable runs, and a visible accessibility payoff. Lesson for Ridge: find a museum, library or farm that will actually install what you build.3 21
Public contact
Project partner: Addison Gallery of American Art, addison.andover.edu · addison@andover.edu · (978) 749-4015 (institution, not a person).
